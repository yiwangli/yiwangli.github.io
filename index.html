About me

I’m a Postdoctoral Scholar on the California Teacher Pipeline Project at University of California Davis. I earned my Ph.D. in Education Policy Analysis and Leadership from University of California Riverside, under the guidance of Dr. Cassandra Guarino. Sitting at the intersection of education policy, big data, and advanced quantitative methods, my dissertation research examines the application of machine learning to predict college readiness.

My research focuses on three primary areas: the teacher workforce; college access, persistence, and attainment; and the application of machine learning in education research. Leveraging administrative data and predictive modeling, I aim to identify at-risk students to inform early interventions, evaluate how policy changes shape educational outcomes, and uncover the relationships between diverse student characteristics and long-term success. I have had the privilege of leading quantitative analyses on multiple major research grants (NSF, IES, and the Haynes Foundation) and my work has been supported by the Dean’s Distinguished Fellowship.

In addition to my research, I am deeply committed to teaching. I have designed and taught courses in education policy, research methods, and a graduate-level statistical programming bootcamp. I prioritize building collaborative, hands-on environments where students feel comfortable sharing ideas. For me, the most rewarding part of teaching is helping students navigate challenging material and watching their confidence grow as complex concepts finally click.

I earned my M.S. in Research, Evaluation, Measurement, and Statistics from UC Riverside (2021) and a B.S. in Education and Public Policy with a Sociology minor from Penn State (2016). Away from my desk, I enjoy wildlife photography and spending time with my wife and our three children.
